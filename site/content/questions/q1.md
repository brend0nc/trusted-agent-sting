# Can models persuade other agents to give them points?

What we measure: does talking win points? Same attacker, same rivals — one arm with the message channel (04), one without (05), byte-identical seatings. The score is capture: the share of the rivals' pull budget the attacker wins (report.py `floor_contrast`, 30 episodes per side).

What we found: talk pays for two of three models. gpt-5.5 gains {{questions.q1.floor_contrast.gpt55.contrast:+.3f}} (95% CI [{{questions.q1.floor_contrast.gpt55.ci.0:+.3f}}, {{questions.q1.floor_contrast.gpt55.ci.1:+.3f}}]). qwen3.7-max gains {{questions.q1.floor_contrast.qwen37max.contrast:+.3f}} (CI [{{questions.q1.floor_contrast.qwen37max.ci.0:+.3f}}, {{questions.q1.floor_contrast.qwen37max.ci.1:+.3f}}]). Both survive Benjamini-Hochberg correction over the registered contrast family — the firmest results here. opus-4.8's {{questions.q1.floor_contrast.opus48.contrast:+.3f}} does not survive it: not settled either way. Raw points would mislead: with messages off every turn is a pull, so everyone's points rise. An earlier points-unit headline ("talking did not pay") was retracted for that artifact.

Limits: the rivals are one cooperative model, glm-5.2. It pays back {{questions.q1.payback_floor.observed.value:.0%}} of pulls anyway (n = {{questions.q1.payback_floor.observed.n}} episodes), against a tested chance floor of {{questions.q1.payback_floor.floor.value:.0%}} (permuted-beneficiary null, metrics.payback_chance_floor). A real gain, but a small one, against an easy crowd. Nothing here says how talk fares against tougher targets. Next: the mixed economy (07) puts a price on every action.
